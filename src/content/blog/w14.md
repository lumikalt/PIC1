# Log

This week, we progressed with the graphical visualization of sensor data. We continued testing the magnetorquers and realized that the initial coils needed more windings to produce a more visible effect. We then used thinner copper wire to wind a square coil, which allowed us to observe the suspended cube reacting when a magnet was brought close to it.

> Magnetorquer working

We realized that the larger battery already included protection against excessive discharge, which allowed us to reconsider the use of the CN3065, as it is more suitable for managing charging through the solar panel.

We also attempted to integrate a current sensor to measure the current supplied by the solar panels and use that data in a future graphical interface. However, we found that the sensor we purchased could only measure currents above around 180 mA, while our solar panel provides a maximum current of approximately 11 mA, making it unsuitable for this application.

# Meetings

In another meeting with the IT professors:

- Discussed the coil tests and concluded that at least two coils placed on perpendicular faces would be needed to allow greater control over the cube’s orientation.
- Discussed the possibility of replacing permanent magnets with an external coil to create a more uniform magnetic field.
- At this stage, we decided not to manufacture a custom PCB designed in KiCad and instead use a prototype board. Nevertheless, the KiCad design work was useful to better understand the required connections and additional components, such as capacitors and resistors.
